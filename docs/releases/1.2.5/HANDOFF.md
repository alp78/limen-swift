# Limen iPhone 1.2.5 (7), the annex by room, the unbounded backups and JPEG XL, 21 October 2026

Written 21 October 2026 against Swift commit `69e7f72` (the 1.2.2 (6) TestFlight notes) and Android `origin/main` at `94c7cf7`, the head of 1.2.5 (versionCode 10), which carries 1.2.3 (versionCode 8, the annex by room and the end of every size limit), 1.2.4 (versionCode 9, the plates as JPEG at 200 dpi and the streamed cipher) and 1.2.5 (the JPEG XL repacking). The request is `_scratch/ios-annex-backups-1.2.5.md`; it replaces `_scratch/ios-photo-annex.md`, which was never carried out here, and the two are kept as the record of what was asked. The Android checkout at `/Users/aperi/DEV/ANDROID/limen` was already at `94c7cf7` and clean; nothing in the Android repository or on the website was modified.

Two things the owner asked to be reported before anything else:

- **The 10 MB stop line was crossed, and the cause is the encoder, not the layout.** Thirty 12-megapixel photographs made a 14.0 MB PDF at ImageIO's quality 0.85, where Android makes 7.7 MB at libjpeg's 85. The two scales are not the same curve: on the same plates, ImageIO 0.85 writes 1.75 times libjpeg 85's bytes with a luminance quantiser three times finer, and ImageIO 0.60 writes 0.97 times them with a slightly finer table (mean quantiser 15.8 against 17.3). `AnnexPlate.jpegQuality` is 0.60, Android's plate rather than Android's number, and the same thirty photographs make an 8.06 MB PDF, 31 pages, under the line. The measurement, the calibration table and the decision are in [evidence/jpeg-quality-calibration.txt](evidence/jpeg-quality-calibration.txt); reverting is one constant.
- **Core Graphics does not re-encode the plates.** Each plate is written once as a JPEG into a spool and handed to the PDF context through a JPEG data provider, and `pdfimages -all` returns the same bytes from the PDF, so Android's `PdfPlates` swap has no counterpart here. The export test asserts it on every plate, and `pdfimages -list` of the measured document shows thirty `jpeg` streams at 200 dpi and nothing else but the seal and the two inks.

The third stop condition, libjxl not building, did not arise: v0.12.0 with Brotli, Highway and skcms fetched at their pinned hashes built for arm64 iOS and the arm64 simulator in one pass.

## What changed

**The photo annex** is Android 1.2.4's photographic report. A contents page after the body lists each section with its photograph count and its first page, which a counting pass with no output works out from the photographs' headers and every legend's wrap. Then one section per room in the order of the rooms, opening on its own page under the room's name in capitals over a heavy rule: the room's overview photographs first, then its findings in the body's numbering, then the objects in that room; roomless objects and findings, orphaned findings, the meters and anything left follow under their own titles. Every photograph sits large in a rounded frame, up to the full width or 1100 units high; the legend sits beside a tall photograph when 300 units are free to its right and under it otherwise, and repeats the room, the finding's number badge and words as the body prints them, the surface, who is held to it and the tenant's response, then the photograph's index in its owner, when it was taken or imported in Europe/Zurich, and its file name. A page that continues a section centres its cards so a single photograph does not hang from the top. No hash anywhere on a plate: it tells the people at a handover nothing. `AnnexContent` and `AnnexPlate` carry Android's sections and the size rule, fit to 1020 by 1100 units at four thirds of a pixel per unit, 200 dpi, never enlarged. `RecordContent` lost its photograph list and prints an attachment as three lines, as Android does.

**The document is files, not memory.** `PdfExport.document` writes the PDF through a `CGPDFContext` on a file and the HTML companion through a file-backed `HtmlSink` in the same drawing pass, spools each plate once as a JPEG, embeds it in the PDF as that DCT stream and in the HTML as the same bytes, validates the PDF through PDFKit and hands back the two paths; the preview moves them into place and the export copies them into the retained folder and the public one. The old whole-document `Data` is gone, which is what lets a thirty-photograph record export in four seconds on the simulator without holding the plates twice.

**Every size limit went.** The record JSON reader's 8 MiB guard (regenerated out of `ProtocolJSON.swift` through `tools/schema.py`), the archive's 8 MiB, 64 MiB, 256 MiB and 20,000-entry bounds, the 32 MiB and 80-megapixel intake bounds, the 32,768-pixel decode cap, and the copy that named them. A photograph is accepted when it decodes and a document when it opens. The ZIP writer streams each entry from its file, patches the local header in place rather than writing a data descriptor (Java's `ZipInputStream` refuses a stored entry with one), and writes ZIP64 records wherever a size, an offset or a count needs them; the reader takes ZIP64 from the central directory, from a local header and from Java's 8-byte descriptors, and inflates an entry in pieces straight to its file, refusing a declared size the inflated bytes pass. The restore and the decrypt staging check the volume's free space once per megabyte and need two megabytes ahead, so a full phone fails with its reason rather than a half-written store. The cipher is unchanged, the LIMENSAFE envelope byte for byte, and was already streaming.

**Backups repack their JPEGs as JPEG XL.** libjxl v0.12.0, Brotli 1.2.0, Highway 1.2.0 and skcms are fetched by `tools/fetch-deps.sh` at the SHA-256 hashes the Android script pins, built by `tools/build-libjxl.sh` with CMake and Ninja for arm64 iOS and the arm64 simulator (MinSizeRel, one Highway target, NEON, JPEG transcoding and the container boxes on), merged into `ThirdParty/LimenJxl.xcframework`, and wrapped by the `LimenJxl` static framework's C shim, the Android `jpeg_xl.cpp` line for line: effort 7, one thread, the JPEG reconstruction data stored. `JpegXL` implements `JpegPacker`, and `JpegXL.loaded` is the default packer of every export and import. A photograph that starts with the JPEG marker, repacks smaller and rebuilds byte for byte goes in stored under `jpegxl/<record>/<file>`; the manifest is written last and says `version=2` when any photograph was repacked, `version=1` otherwise, so a backup with nothing repacked still restores on 1.2.4 and earlier. On restore a `jpegxl/` entry is unpacked into its place, a build without the library refuses a version 2 backup whole, and a repacked photograph that does not rebuild rejects its record and installs nothing of it. `ThirdParty/` is gitignored and `make gen` stops when the framework is missing. The NOTICE carries the four licences as the Android notice does, and the licences screen shows it.

**Memory.** The first measurement peaked at 1.2 GB on the simulator for thirty 12-megapixel photographs. Two causes, both fixed: ImageIO hands back autoreleased buffers the size of the whole photograph, and FileHandle's chunk reads are autoreleased too, while an export is one synchronous call on the main actor whose pool drains only when it returns. Each plate's decode, redraw and encode, each photograph's repacking, each hash and readability check, and each chunk of the ZIP writer, the ZIP reader and the cipher now run in a pool of their own. Measured again on the simulator over the same thirty photographs, the export peaks at 101 MB and comes back to 56 MB, the repacked backup peaks at 283 MB (one photograph, its repacked form and its check decode in flight) and comes back to 57 MB; on the phone the export peaks at 116 MB and the repacking at 650 MB, libjxl's working set for one 12-megapixel JPEG, and each phase comes back to about 54 MB ([measure-footprint.txt](evidence/measure-footprint.txt), [measure-footprint-phone.txt](evidence/measure-footprint-phone.txt)). A third cause showed only on the phone, in the larger-than-memory gate: the sealed restore grew the footprint by the whole archive, 1 GiB for a 1 GiB backup, where the simulator grew by nothing. A probe of every restore step alone pinned it to the decrypt loop, which carried the 16-byte tag window to the next chunk as a `suffix` slice of the chunk it came from; on the phone that slice kept its storage alive and appended into it, so every chunk read stayed resident. The window is a copy of its own now, and the same 1 GiB backup grows the footprint by 1 MB on the phone ([phone-large-backup.txt](evidence/phone-large-backup.txt)). Repacking is also skipped above 64 MiB (`ArchiveIO.repackLimit`): libjxl needs the whole JPEG in memory to transcode it, a JPEG that size is not a photograph a phone took, and it travels as it is; Android reads such a file whole, which is a known divergence.

**The document's file name**, changed on the owner's instruction during the phone run: the address and the handover's day with dashes between (`Avenue-de-la-Mesure-30-2026-10-21.pdf` and its `.html` twin), letters folded to ASCII, nothing else. The language, the fingerprint, the delivery revision, the export time and a random suffix used to follow the address and made the name useless in Files and in a mail; they stay in the record's export history, which is what the app reads. A second export of the same record on the same day is numbered (`-2`, `-3`) against both the retained folder and Documents/Limen, so the history keeps every exact copy. Android's name is longer and is a known divergence. `Exports.documentName` and `Exports.unusedName` carry it.

**Version** is 1.2.5 build 7 in `Project.swift`, which `BundleTests` asserts in the built bundle; the LimenKit framework links `-lc++` for libjxl. Build 6 (1.2.2) was uploaded on 15 October 2026 (delivery `217c7844-67da-40c7-ac78-68e7bea2f70d`) and submitted to Beta App Review on 16 October; the 1.2.2 handoff's "nothing was uploaded", written before that upload, carries a dated correction. Build 7 is built in Release here and not uploaded.

## The phone gate, command by command

With the iPhone unlocked (and on the cable if Wi-Fi keeps refusing the developer disk image), from `/Users/aperi/DEV/SWIFT/limen`:

```bash

xcodebuild -workspace Limen.xcworkspace -scheme AllTests -destination 'id=00008150-001074183A7B401C' -derivedDataPath ./DD build-for-testing
xcodebuild -workspace Limen.xcworkspace -scheme AllTests -destination 'id=00008150-001074183A7B401C' -derivedDataPath ./DD -only-testing:LimenKitTests/JpegXLTests -only-testing:LimenKitTests/ArchiveSizeAndPackingTests test-without-building
```

The second line builds the Debug test bundles for the phone under the audit identity; the third runs the real-libjxl round trip and the archive suite there. For the measurement and the phone screenshots, put thirty of the phone's own photographs, as JPEG, into the audit app's Documents (AirDrop them to the phone, then Files, On My iPhone, the Limen audit app's folder, a `measure` folder; or `xcrun devicectl device copy to --device 00008150-001074183A7B401C --domain-type appDataContainer --domain-identifier dev.peringer.limen.audit --source <file> --destination Documents/measure/<file>` per file), then:

```bash

xcodebuild -workspace Limen.xcworkspace -scheme AllTests -destination 'id=00008150-001074183A7B401C' -derivedDataPath ./DD -only-testing:LimenTests/MeasurementTests test-without-building
unset TEST_RUNNER_LIMEN_MEASURE_PHOTOS TEST_RUNNER_LIMEN_MEASURE_OUT TEST_RUNNER_LIMEN_MEASURE_KEEP
xcodebuild -workspace Limen.xcworkspace -scheme AllTests -destination 'id=00008150-001074183A7B401C' -derivedDataPath ./DD -only-testing:LimenUITests/AnnexShotsTest test-without-building && make shots
```
